About the training

Why do some people and organizations keep producing fresh ideas while others repeat the same routines year after year? This training answers that question in plain language, with no background in psychology or management needed.

Creativity is the ability to produce ideas that are both new and useful. Innovation is the work of turning such an idea into something that is tested, accepted, and used. An organization needs both, and both can be learned and managed.

Every theory and every step is connected to daily work, such as handling requests, writing letters, serving clients, keeping records, or caring for patients.

By the end you will be able to

  • Explain seven theories of creativity and give a workplace example for each.
  • Describe the five steps of the creativity process and the five steps of the innovation process.
  • Use practical tools such as problem questions, scoring matrices, SCAMPER, prototypes, and test plans.
  • Name the habits and conditions that help or block new ideas in an office.
  • Present one real workplace idea, from problem to rollout plan, in two minutes.

The seven theories

Three theories look at the environment, two at the process of creating, and two at the person. A creative organization pays attention to all three at once. Open a theory to see its central question, its main idea, and its activity.

1.1.1 Shrinking Earth Theory20 min, environment

How does a connected world push people and organizations to create?

As roads, airlines, mobile networks, and the internet shrink distance, ideas, people, and information meet more often. That meeting of different ideas is a strong source of creativity. Mobile money, for example, joined a bank account with a mobile phone, and telebirr changed how many Ethiopians pay and send money.

What you can do
Learn from others, and adapt their ideas to your own context instead of copying them blindly.
Activity 1, Idea Import (10 minutes). Think of a good practice from another office, university, or country. Say what problem it could solve for you and what must change so that it fits your culture.
1.1.2 Systems Theory25 min, environment

How do the person, the field of experts, and the knowledge domain work together?

Developed mainly by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi. Creativity happens in the interaction of three parts. The domain is the body of knowledge and rules. The field is the people who decide what is accepted, such as supervisors and committees. The individual is the person who brings a new variation. An idea becomes a creative contribution when the field accepts it.

What you can do
Master your domain, understand how the field judges ideas, and think about who will be affected by your change.
Activity 2, Map the System (15 minutes). In groups of four, draw a routine process, mark who must approve each step, and circle one step to improve.
1.1.3 Honing Theory25 min, process

How do ideas and the thinker change through repeated refinement?

Developed by Liane Gabora. Ideas rarely arrive complete. They are sharpened through many drafts, and each draft teaches the creator something new. The idea changes the thinker, and the thinker changes the idea. The visitor register example shows four drafts that end in a smooth process and a much deeper understanding of visitors.

What you can do
Start rough, ask for feedback early, keep earlier versions, and ask after each draft what it taught you.
Activity 3, Three Drafts (15 minutes). Improve a form, notice, or agenda in three rounds of three minutes, with a neighbor commenting after each round.
1.1.4 Everyday Imaginative Thought Theory25 min, person

Is creativity only for geniuses or part of everyday life?

Creativity grows from ordinary imagination. The Four C model of Kaufman and Beghetto describes a ladder from mini-c (a personal insight) and little-c (everyday work creativity) to pro-c (professional) and Big-C (historic achievements). Most valuable workplace innovation comes from many small improvements by ordinary people.

What you can do
Ask what if questions about daily tasks, reverse assumptions, work with constraints, and imagine the process as the client sees it.
Activity 4, Five What-Ifs (10 minutes). Pick one weekly task, write five what if questions about it, and share your most interesting one.
1.1.5 Evolution Theory25 min, process

How do many ideas compete and the best survive?

Proposed by Donald Campbell and developed by Dean Keith Simonton. Creativity works through variation (many different ideas), selection (testing them against criteria and in small pilots), and retention (making what works standard practice). Nobody can know in advance which idea will succeed, so quantity is one of the roads to quality.

What you can do
Generate many ideas, do not criticize too early, pilot the best, and standardize what works.
Activity 5, Idea Tournament (15 minutes). List ideas for improving service in five minutes, pick the best three by useful, feasible, and affordable, then plan a small test for the winner.
1.1.6 Investment Theory30 min, person

Why do creative people choose unpopular ideas and what resources do they need?

Proposed by Robert Sternberg and Todd Lubart. Creative people buy low and sell high with ideas. They pick ideas that are unknown or doubted, develop them, persuade others, and then move on. Six resources work together: intellectual abilities, knowledge, thinking styles, personality, motivation, and environment.

What you can do
Develop your six resources, and treat evidence, clear communication, and patience as part of the investment.
Activity 6, Resource Check (15 minutes). Rate yourself from 1 to 5 on each resource, choose one to strengthen, and write one concrete step.
1.1.7 Social Personality Theory30 min, environment

How do personality traits and the social setting shape creativity?

Creativity comes from the meeting of personal traits, such as curiosity, independence, and persistence, with a supportive environment. Teresa Amabile found that people are usually more creative when motivated by interest and challenge than when pressured by threats, strict surveillance, or heavy competition. Culture matters too, because junior staff may hesitate to share ideas where respect for seniority is strong.

What you can do
Protect intrinsic motivation and create safe ways to speak, such as short idea meetings and thanking people who raise problems.
Activity 7, Climate Check (15 minutes). List three things in your office that help creativity and three that block it, then choose one blocker you can influence and write an action.

Your action plan

Activity 8, My Creativity Commitments (15 minutes). Finish three sentences. From one theory, what you will start doing by a date. From another theory, what you will stop or change by a date. And what you will do to help your team at the next meeting. Be specific and realistic.
